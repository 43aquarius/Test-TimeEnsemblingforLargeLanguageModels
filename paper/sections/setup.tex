\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Benchmarks}

We assemble a six-benchmark suite that spans knowledge, commonsense, mathematical reasoning, and logical reasoning, with a mix of option counts and answer formats. TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} contributes adversarial factual questions with four or five options; we shuffle option order per question to avoid position bias. PIQA \citep{bisk2020piqa} contributes two-choice physical commonsense problems. LogiQA \citep{liu2020logiqa} contributes four-choice logical reading comprehension. LSAT logical reasoning and SAT mathematics, both from the AGIEval suite \citep{zhong2023agieval}, contribute five-choice analytical reasoning and four-choice quantitative items respectively. GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021training} contributes free-form grade-school mathematics problems answered with chain-of-thought reasoning and a final numeric value. We sample fifteen questions per benchmark uniformly at random with a fixed seed, giving ninety questions in total. The mixture is deliberately diverse in difficulty: as Section~\ref{sec:results} shows, the strongest models in our pool saturate five of the six benchmarks while all models find LogiQA and, for some models, GSM8K and SAT mathematics challenging, which supplies both the degenerate and the high-entropy regimes that our analysis requires.

\subsection{Models}

We study six commercial models from four families, accessed through three providers under their free tiers: Qwen3.8-Max and Qwen3.8-Omni from the Qwen family \citep{yang2025qwen3,qwen2024qwen}; Agnes3.0-Flash and Agnes2.5-Flash from the Agnes family \citep{agnesai2026}; GPT-6-Luna from the GPT family \citep{openai2023gpt4,openai2025gpt5}; and Gemini3.8-Flash from the Gemini family \citep{comanici2025gemini}. The models span a range of capability tiers and post-training styles, including one model that emits an explicit private reasoning trace and one multimodal variant. All models are queried through OpenAI-compatible endpoints; the main-suite data was collected on October 17, 2026, and the follow-up conditions on October 18, 2026. Sampling uses temperature $0.7$ with default nucleus probability; greedy calls use temperature $0$. For the two Qwen models we disable the proprietary thinking mode on multiple-choice tasks to control token expenditure and enable it on GSM8K, where chain-of-thought is necessary; all other models run in their default configurations, and every model receives the identical instruction format described below. Because the models are served through third-party endpoints, we cannot verify whether the provider honors the requested temperature for reasoning-style models; the Qwen thinking mode is the one decoding parameter we control explicitly, and we report per-call token usage and timestamps in the released logs so that the decoding configuration is fully auditable. Table~\ref{tab:models} states the exact model identifiers, the access dates, and the call counts.

\subsection{Prompt design and paraphrase generation}

The canonical prompt for a multiple-choice question consists of the passage when present, the question stem, the option list labeled $(A)$ through $(E)$, and a fixed instruction that requires a direct answer in the form \texttt{Answer: <letter>} without explanation. GSM8K prompts instead require step-by-step reasoning ending with \texttt{Answer: <number>}. Direct answering on multiple-choice tasks keeps the comparison uniform across models, keeps token costs bounded, and isolates the aggregation question from chain-of-thought length effects; the GSM8K condition covers the reasoning-trace regime.

Paraphrases target the parts of the prompt where semantic-preserving variation is well defined: the question stem for TruthfulQA, LSAT, and LogiQA, and the instruction wording for GSM8K, PIQA, and SAT mathematics, whose stems contain problem-specific content such as numbers that should not be altered. This design yields two paraphrase loci, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports all fragility and gain statistics stratified by locus. Stem paraphrases are generated by a seventh model (Qwen3.7-Flash) that is not part of the evaluation pool, prompted to rewrite each stem in six ways while preserving all facts, entities, numbers, and the question type. Instruction paraphrases are six handcrafted variants per dataset that vary wording while keeping the literal answer-format requirement fixed, which keeps answer extraction reliable. As a robustness check, a second generator (Agnes3.0-Flash, outside its own evaluation role) regenerates paraphrases for all stem-locus questions, and the alternative set is validated to the same standard; a planned re-evaluation of the prompt-brittle models under the alternative paraphrases was cut short by provider quota exhaustion and is left to future work.

Every generated paraphrase passes a three-stage validation filter. A lexical stage rejects paraphrases that are identical to the original or whose length ratio falls outside $[0.4, 2.5]$. A semantic stage embeds the original stem and each candidate with a text embedding model and rejects candidates whose cosine similarity to the original falls below $0.75$. A form stage, applied to factual questions, rejects rewrites that change the question type, for example from a wh-question to a yes-no question. Candidates that fail any stage are replaced by regenerated candidates until six valid paraphrases per question are obtained; the final set achieves a mean embedding similarity of $0.86$ with the originals. Two further checks strengthen the semantic stage. A bidirectional entailment test, administered by an auxiliary language model, asks whether each paraphrase entails the original and is entailed by it: 267 of 270 final paraphrases pass both directions, and 269 of 270 of the second generator's paraphrases pass. We additionally reviewed one hundred paraphrase--original pairs manually, finding the filter's precision adequate. As a sensitivity analysis, the fragility statistics are recomputed under a stricter embedding threshold of 0.80, and the qualitative pattern is unchanged.

\subsection{Execution and quality control}

The experiment executes the sixteen-call matrix of Section~\ref{sec:method} for every question and model through an asynchronous runner with per-provider rate limiting, automatic retry with exponential backoff, and append-only checkpointing. Answer extraction uses the mandated \texttt{Answer:} format first and falls back to scanning the final output lines for a well-formed option letter or number, with the option range constrained by the question. Every stored record includes the extracted answer, its correctness, token usage, and a content snippet for auditing. Extraction succeeds on between 94.7\% and 100\% of calls across the six models; cells whose first decode fails to parse are re-drawn under the identical prompt and decoding configuration until extraction succeeds or the free-tier quota of the provider is exhausted, and questions with any remaining unparsed cell are excluded from that model's analysis, so all reported accuracies are computed over complete question matrices. Two safeguards address the residual exclusions. First, a sensitivity analysis recomputes every condition under a lenient rule that aggregates whichever votes parsed, requiring at least four of six; the qualitative ordering of conditions is unchanged. Second, the excluded questions are compared with the included ones on greedy accuracy to probe for difficulty-related selection bias; no systematic difference in difficulty is observed (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Per-model completed question counts, which vary between 12 and 90 owing to provider rate limits and quota, are stated alongside every result, and pooled analyses use the intersection of completed questions across the models involved, with question-level cluster bootstrap intervals.

\subsection{Follow-up conditions}

Three follow-up conditions extend the main matrix; all reuse its prompts, paraphrases, and parsing.

\textbf{Greedy paraphrases.} For every model and every question in its completed set, each of the six paraphrases is decoded once at $\tau = 0$. The control yields the noise-free paraphrase ensemble $\mathrm{PE}^{g}\text{-}6$ and the noise-free fragility indicator of Section~\ref{sec:method}, and it covers five of the six models (the free-tier quota of Qwen3.8-Omni was exhausted after completing its main matrix).

\textbf{Hard-task probe.} Because the flagship models saturate most of the main suite, we probe a harder distribution: thirty questions sampled uniformly at random (fixed seed, stratified by subject) from the MMLU-Pro test set \citep{wang2024mmlupro}, which offers ten options per question. The probe administers the single-call greedy baseline and the six-sample self-consistency condition per question, without paraphrases, under the direct-answering instruction. GPT-6-Luna and the two Agnes models cover fifteen, thirteen, and eight complete questions respectively; on hard quantitative items the direct-answering instruction does not always suppress reasoning, and outputs truncated at the token cap lost extraction on a majority of Qwen3.8-Max's probe calls, leaving six complete questions (eight with at least four parsed votes) before its quota was exhausted.

\textbf{Chain-of-thought answering.} The main suite answers multiple-choice questions directly, while self-consistency was originally motivated for chain-of-thought reasoning \citep{wei2022chain,wang2023selfconsistency}. The follow-up therefore repeats the greedy baseline, the six-sample self-consistency condition, and, for stem-locus questions, the six-paraphrase ensemble under an explicit instruction to reason step by step and end with the mandated answer format. The CoT condition covers the full multiple-choice sets of Agnes3.0-Flash, Agnes2.5-Flash, and GPT-6-Luna, a twelve-question stratified subset for Qwen3.8-Max, and a fifteen-question subset planned for Qwen3.8-Omni whose free-tier quota was exhausted before the condition could run; Gemini3.8-Flash is excluded because its small completed set left no provider capacity after the greedy-paraphrase control. GSM8K is excluded because its main-suite condition is already chain-of-thought.

\subsection{Conditions and metrics}

Each model and question yields the greedy baseline, $\mathrm{SC}\text{-}k$ and $\mathrm{PE}\text{-}k$ for $k \in \{2, 4, 6\}$, the hybrid $\mathrm{HB}\text{-}6$, the combined $\mathrm{COMB}\text{-}12$, the agreement statistics $c_{\mathrm{S}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{P}}$, the support-normalized answer entropy, the single-sample baseline $\mathrm{S1}$, and the fragility indicator. Accuracy is the fraction of questions answered correctly. Error detection is evaluated by AUROC, AURC, and E-AURC as defined in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Budget curves plot accuracy against $k$ for both pure strategies. All analysis code operates on the raw prediction logs, and we release the logs together with the prompts and paraphrases.
