\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Principal findings}

The study yields four principal findings. First, the effectiveness of every test-time aggregation strategy is governed by a precondition that the literature rarely states explicitly: the model must produce diverse answers at all. For the flagship models in our pool, five of six benchmarks sit in a degenerate regime in which six stochastic samples and six paraphrases return the same answer for almost every question, and in that regime self-consistency, paraphrase ensembling, and the combined ensemble reproduce the greedy baseline exactly, with the hybrid allocation differing by at most one question. Second, in the high-entropy regime, which in our suite is concentrated in logical reasoning for weaker models and in selected quantitative tasks for mid-tier models, both pure strategies improve over greedy decoding when the correct answer is the modal one, while the highest-entropy cells show that majority voting can leave accuracy unchanged or even reduce it when the modal answer is wrong. Third, paraphrase agreement functions as an error signal in its own right: across models, its AUROC for predicting ensemble errors is comparable to that of sample agreement in the regimes where both vary, and the stronger source differs across models, so neither signal subsumes the other and a deployment that can afford both probes obtains complementary evidence. Fourth, fragility, the fraction of questions whose answer flips under paraphrasing alone, tracks answer entropy and concentrates in the same model-task cells that benefit from ensembling, which supports the interpretation that paraphrase disagreement and sampling disagreement are two observable symptoms of the same underlying instability.

\subsection{A practical allocation rule}

The regime structure suggests a simple procedure for practitioners with a fixed per-question budget. Probe the question first with a small number of calls, compute sample agreement and paraphrase agreement, and branch on the result. If both agreement scores equal one, additional calls of either kind are unlikely to change the answer, and the budget is better spent elsewhere; the degenerate regime is identifiable from a handful of calls at negligible cost. If sample agreement is high but paraphrase agreement is low, the model is prompt-brittle, and the remaining budget should go to paraphrases. If the reverse holds, the budget should go to resampling. If both scores are low, the hybrid split performs at least as well as either pure strategy in our data and hedges against misjudging which source is more informative. Because agreement statistics are cheap to compute online, the rule adds no model training and no human labeling.

\subsection{Implications for evaluation and for uncertainty estimation}

Two methodological consequences follow. For benchmark evaluation, our results add an aggregation dimension to the multi-prompt argument of \citet{mizrahi2024state}: the same question answered under one prompt with one decode, under six paraphrases, or under twelve combined calls yields systematically different accuracies in the high-entropy regime, so reported scores should state the aggregation configuration, and comparisons across papers that aggregate differently are not like for like. For uncertainty estimation, the degenerate regime is a warning. Sampling-based confidence measures, including semantic entropy \citep{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024detecting} and simple agreement heuristics, are uninformative when the sample distribution collapses, and our data show that this collapse is the common case for strong models on saturated tasks, precisely the setting in which hallucination detection is often deployed. Paraphrase probes partially restore the signal because they perturb the input rather than the decoder, but they too inherit the collapse when the model is robust in both views, as our flagship models are on five of six benchmarks. Confidence methods should therefore be validated per regime rather than averaged over mixed test sets, and deployments should verify that the answer distribution carries variance before trusting agreement-based scores.

\subsection{Limitations}

Five limitations bound the scope of our conclusions. First, provider rate limits constrained three of the six models to between roughly one and two thirds of the ninety-question suite; all comparisons within a model are paired and valid on the completed subset, but cross-model aggregates weight models unequally, and we state per-model sample sizes throughout. Second, the benchmark suite mixes difficulty levels unevenly: the flagship models saturate most datasets, so the high-entropy regime is sampled through fewer model-task cells than the degenerate regime, and our conclusions about that regime rest primarily on logical reasoning and selected quantitative tasks. Third, paraphrases were generated by a single auxiliary model and validated by embedding similarity with fixed thresholds; other generators or stricter semantic equivalence checks could shift the fragility statistics, although the two paraphrase loci we study behave consistently. Fourth, the multiple-choice tasks use direct answering to keep token costs and format effects controlled, while GSM8K uses chain-of-thought; the aggregation comparison is internally valid within each regime, but absolute accuracies are not comparable across the two answering modes. Fifth, all models are commercial systems accessed through third-party endpoints whose decoding internals, post-training, and version pinning are outside our control; findings describe the accessed systems as of October 2026 and need not transfer to other versions or serving configurations.

\subsection{Future work}

Three extensions follow naturally. The allocation rule can be made adaptive per question, spending calls sequentially until an agreement threshold is reached, which connects our offline comparison to the early-stopping literature \citep{aggarwal2023lets}. The comparison can be extended to free-form generation, where semantic clustering replaces majority voting and paraphrase ensembles interact with surface-form competition effects \citep{holtzman2021surface}. Finally, the fragility statistic can serve as a benchmark diagnostic in its own right, complementing accuracy with a measure of how strongly a model's answers depend on the surface form of the question.
