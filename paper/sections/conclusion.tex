\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a budget-matched comparison of the two diversity sources available to test-time ensembling for large language models: resampling the decoder on a fixed prompt, and perturbing the prompt across validated semantic paraphrases. A sixteen-call matrix per question per model, evaluated on six models from four families and six benchmarks, allowed every condition, including self-consistency, paraphrase ensembling, a hybrid allocation, and a combined ensemble, to be derived from matched calls on identical questions. The results organize into a regime structure. Where models are confident, answer distributions collapse, all aggregation strategies coincide with greedy decoding, and consistency-based uncertainty estimation loses its signal. Where answer entropy exists, both diversity sources improve accuracy, paraphrase agreement complements sample agreement as an error detector, and a hybrid allocation matches or outperforms the pure strategies. The practical takeaway is to measure agreement first and allocate the budget accordingly, and the methodological takeaway is that aggregation configuration belongs in the reporting standard of every evaluation that uses it. We release prompts, paraphrases, and raw predictions to support replication as model behavior continues to shift.
