\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Benchmarks}

We assemble a six-benchmark suite that spans knowledge, commonsense, mathematical reasoning, and logical reasoning, with a mix of option counts and answer formats. TruthfulQA-MC1 \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} contributes adversarial factual questions with four or five options; we shuffle option order per question to avoid position bias. PIQA \citep{bisk2020piqa} contributes two-choice physical commonsense problems. LogiQA \citep{liu2020logiqa} contributes four-choice logical reading comprehension. LSAT logical reasoning and SAT mathematics, both from the AGIEval suite \citep{zhong2023agieval}, contribute five-choice analytical reasoning and four-choice quantitative items respectively. GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021training} contributes free-form grade-school mathematics problems answered with chain-of-thought reasoning and a final numeric value. We sample fifteen questions per benchmark uniformly at random with a fixed seed, giving ninety questions in total. For the replication study of Section~\ref{sec:expand}, a second draw of 119 fresh questions (twenty per benchmark, nineteen for LSAT logical reasoning) is taken from the same raw pools with the same filters by replaying the seeded shuffle and skipping consumed items, so the two sets share a construction procedure and nothing else. The mixture is deliberately diverse in difficulty: as Section~\ref{sec:results} shows, the strongest models in our pool saturate five of the six benchmarks while all models find LogiQA and, for some models, GSM8K and SAT mathematics challenging, which supplies both the degenerate and the high-entropy regimes that our analysis requires.

\subsection{Models and suites}

The study evaluates ten commercial systems from five families, accessed through three providers under their free tiers and organized into two question suites with distinct roles (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The \emph{development suite} (90 questions, collected October 17--18, 2026) covers six systems: Qwen3.8-Max and Qwen3.8-Omni from the Qwen family \citep{yang2025qwen3,qwen2024qwen} on the Aliyun MaaS endpoint; Agnes3.0-Flash and Agnes2.5-Flash from the Agnes family \citep{agnesai2026} on the Agnes API endpoint; GPT-6-Luna from the GPT family \citep{openai2023gpt4,openai2025gpt5} and Gemini3.8-Flash from the Gemini family \citep{google2026gemini38}, both on the Apinex endpoint. Its per-model coverage was truncated unevenly by free-tier quotas, to between 12 and 90 complete question matrices. The \emph{held-out suite} (119 fresh questions, collected October 19--23, 2026) re-evaluates the two Agnes models on fresh items and adds four DeepSeek-family systems \citep{deepseek2026api} and Qwen3.7-Flash: DeepSeekV4.1-Flash, first attempted on Aliyun MaaS (9 questions, retained as a provider record) and completed on the Apinex endpoint; DeepSeekV4-Pro-0813, a pro-tier sibling exposed by the same endpoint; DeepSeekV4-Flash-0731, stopped at 13 questions when its endpoint moved it behind a paid subscription; and Qwen3.7-Flash, stopped at 60 questions when its provider revoked free-tier access. Four systems complete all 119 questions and carry the primary held-out analysis; the two partial systems are reported separately and excluded from tabulation. The models span capability tiers from fast to flagship, including one model that emits an explicit private reasoning trace and one multimodal variant. All systems are queried through OpenAI-compatible endpoints in direct-answer mode on multiple-choice tasks and chain-of-thought on GSM8K; for the two Qwen flagships the proprietary thinking mode is disabled on multiple-choice tasks and enabled on GSM8K. Sampling uses temperature $0.7$ with default nucleus probability; greedy calls use temperature $0$. Exact endpoint model identifiers, per-call timestamps, and token usage are recorded in the released logs. Because the models are served through third-party endpoints --- Aliyun MaaS, the Agnes API, and the Apinex relay, the last an aggregator that served GPT-6-Luna, Gemini3.8-Flash, and the completed DeepSeek runs --- we can neither verify that a relay serves the requested model identity and version against the provider's first-party API nor confirm that the requested temperature is honored, especially for reasoning-style models; the Qwen thinking mode is the one decoding parameter we control explicitly, no condition in this study was replicated on a first-party API or an open-weight model, and findings therefore describe the accessed endpoints as of October 2026. We cite each provider's published technical report or model card where one exists and release the exact endpoint model identifiers, per-call timestamps, token usage, and raw outputs, so that the accessed configuration is auditable even where it cannot be verified. Table~\ref{tab:models} states families, endpoints, access dates, parse rates, and completed coverage per suite.

\subsection{Prompt design and paraphrase generation}

The canonical prompt for a multiple-choice question consists of the passage when present, the question stem, the option list labeled $(A)$ through $(E)$, and a fixed instruction that requires a direct answer in the form \texttt{Answer: <letter>} without explanation. GSM8K prompts instead require step-by-step reasoning ending with \texttt{Answer: <number>}. Direct answering on multiple-choice tasks keeps the comparison uniform across models, keeps token costs bounded, and isolates the aggregation question from chain-of-thought length effects; the GSM8K condition covers the reasoning-trace regime.

Paraphrases target the parts of the prompt where semantic-preserving variation is well defined: the question stem for TruthfulQA, LSAT, and LogiQA, and the instruction wording for GSM8K, PIQA, and SAT mathematics, whose stems contain problem-specific content such as numbers that should not be altered. This design yields two paraphrase loci, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports all fragility and gain statistics stratified by locus. Stem paraphrases are generated by Qwen3.7-Flash, prompted to rewrite each stem in six ways while preserving all facts, entities, numbers, and the question type; in the development suite this generator is not part of the evaluation pool. The held-out suite later evaluates Qwen3.7-Flash as a target system (Section~\ref{sec:expand}), a dual role we flag here and wherever its rows appear: its paraphrase-condition results carry a self-preference risk, since the model judges its own rewrites, and its row is moreover a partial replication (60 of 119 questions) excluded from the pooled analyses. Instruction paraphrases are six handcrafted variants per dataset that vary wording while keeping the literal answer-format requirement fixed, which keeps answer extraction reliable. As a robustness check, a second generator (Agnes3.0-Flash, outside its own evaluation role) regenerates paraphrases for all stem-locus questions, and the alternative set is validated to the same standard; a planned re-evaluation of the prompt-brittle models under the alternative paraphrases was cut short by provider quota exhaustion and is left to future work.

Every generated paraphrase passes a three-stage validation filter. A lexical stage rejects paraphrases that are identical to the original or whose length ratio falls outside $[0.4, 2.5]$. A semantic stage embeds the original stem and each candidate with a text embedding model and rejects candidates whose cosine similarity to the original falls below $0.75$. A form stage, applied to factual questions, rejects rewrites that change the question type, for example from a wh-question to a yes-no question. Candidates that fail any stage are replaced by regenerated candidates until six valid paraphrases per question are obtained; the final set achieves a mean embedding similarity of $0.86$ with the originals. One further check strengthens the semantic stage: a bidirectional entailment test, administered by an auxiliary language model, asks whether each paraphrase entails the original and is entailed by it; 267 of 270 final paraphrases pass both directions, and 269 of 270 of the second generator's paraphrases pass, with the full per-item verdicts released alongside the paraphrases. As a sensitivity analysis, the fragility statistics are recomputed under a stricter embedding threshold of 0.80, and the qualitative pattern is unchanged.

\subsection{Execution and quality control}

The experiment executes the sixteen-call matrix of Section~\ref{sec:method} for every question and model through an asynchronous runner with per-provider rate limiting, automatic retry with exponential backoff, and append-only checkpointing. Answer extraction uses the mandated \texttt{Answer:} format first and falls back to scanning the final output lines for a well-formed option letter or number, with the option range constrained by the question. Every stored record includes the extracted answer, its correctness, token usage, and a content snippet for auditing. Extraction succeeds on between 94.7\% and 100\% of calls across the ten systems (Table~\ref{tab:models}); cells whose first decode fails to parse are re-drawn under the identical prompt and decoding configuration until extraction succeeds or the free-tier quota of the provider is exhausted, and questions with any remaining unparsed cell are excluded from that model's analysis, so all reported accuracies are computed over complete question matrices. Two safeguards address the residual exclusions. First, a sensitivity analysis recomputes the ensemble conditions under a lenient rule that admits any question with at least four parsed votes per source and aggregates whichever votes parsed; the ordering of the ensemble conditions is preserved for every model, and the denominators grow (for example, from 33 to 42 and 39 questions on the two sources for Agnes3.0-Flash, whose lenient SC-6 and PE-6 reach 88.1\% and 92.3\% against 90.9\% and 90.9\% on the strict set); because the added questions were disproportionately ones the provider cut mid-run, they are harder on average, so absolute accuracies on the extended sets sit up to four points below their strict-set values. Second, the excluded questions are compared with the included ones on greedy accuracy to probe for difficulty-related selection bias; no systematic difference in the direction of easier exclusions is observed (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Per-model completed question counts, which vary between 12 and 90 on the development suite, are stated alongside every result, and pooled analyses use the intersection of completed questions across the models involved, with question-level cluster bootstrap intervals.

\subsection{Follow-up conditions}

Three follow-up conditions extend the main matrix; all reuse its prompts, paraphrases, and parsing, and all three are analyzed in \ref{app:greedypara}--\ref{app:cot} as descriptive robustness checks rather than primary evidence.

\textbf{Greedy paraphrases.} For every model and every question in its completed set, each of the six paraphrases is decoded once at $\tau = 0$. The control yields the noise-free paraphrase ensemble $\mathrm{PE}^{g}\text{-}6$ and the noise-free fragility indicator of Section~\ref{sec:method}; it covers five of the six development-suite models (the free-tier quota of Qwen3.8-Omni was exhausted after completing its main matrix) and was not re-collected on the held-out suite, whose remaining provider budget went to the fresh-question matrices. Held-out paraphrase conditions therefore retain the decoding-noise mixing that this control removes, and are read with that caveat (\ref{app:greedypara}).

\textbf{Hard-task probe.} Because the flagship models saturate most of the main suite, we probe a harder distribution: thirty questions sampled uniformly at random (fixed seed, stratified by subject) from the MMLU-Pro test set \citep{wang2024mmlupro}, which offers ten options per question. The probe administers the single-call greedy baseline and the six-sample self-consistency condition per question, without paraphrases, under the direct-answering instruction. GPT-6-Luna, Agnes2.5-Flash, and Agnes3.0-Flash cover fifteen, thirteen, and eight complete questions respectively; on hard quantitative items the direct-answering instruction does not always suppress reasoning, and outputs truncated at the token cap lost extraction on a majority of Qwen3.8-Max's probe calls, leaving six complete questions (eight with at least four parsed votes) before its quota was exhausted.

\textbf{Chain-of-thought answering.} The main suite answers multiple-choice questions directly, while self-consistency was originally motivated for chain-of-thought reasoning \citep{wei2022chain,wang2023selfconsistency}. The follow-up therefore repeats the greedy baseline, the six-sample self-consistency condition, and, for stem-locus questions, the six-paraphrase ensemble under an explicit instruction to reason step by step and end with the mandated answer format. The CoT condition covers the full multiple-choice sets of Agnes3.0-Flash, Agnes2.5-Flash, and GPT-6-Luna, a twelve-question stratified subset for Qwen3.8-Max, and a fifteen-question subset planned for Qwen3.8-Omni whose free-tier quota was exhausted before the condition could run; Gemini3.8-Flash is excluded because its small completed set left no provider capacity after the greedy-paraphrase control. GSM8K is excluded because its main-suite condition is already chain-of-thought.

\subsection{Conditions and metrics}

Each model and question yields the greedy reference baseline, the single-sample baseline $\mathrm{S1}$, $\mathrm{SC}\text{-}k$ and $\mathrm{PE}\text{-}k$ for $k \in \{2, 4, 6\}$, the hybrid $\mathrm{HB}\text{-}6$, the combined $\mathrm{COMB}\text{-}12$, the agreement statistics $c_{\mathrm{S}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{P}}$, the modal vote share, and the fragility indicator. Accuracy is the fraction of questions answered correctly. Error detection is evaluated by AUROC and tie-randomized AURC as defined in Section~\ref{sec:method}. Budget curves plot accuracy against $k$ for both pure strategies. All analysis code operates on the raw prediction logs, and we release the logs together with the prompts and paraphrases.
