\title{When Does Test-Time Ensembling Help Large Language Models? A Budget-Matched Study of Answer-Space and Prompt-Space Diversity}

\author[inst1]{Chang Tan\corref{cor1}}
\ead{aquars43@foxmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[inst1]{Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, Liaoning, China}

\begin{abstract}
Test-time ensembling for large language models draws diversity from two sources: resampling the stochastic decoder on a fixed prompt (answer space) or aggregating semantically equivalent paraphrases (prompt space). We compare them budget-matched, answering every question sixteen times per model. A development suite (90 questions, six systems, coverage 12--90 per model after provider-quota truncation) fixed the conditions, strata, and rules; the primary evidence is a held-out suite of 119 fresh questions fully covered by four models from two providers and two families (476 model--question pairs). Against the single-sample expectation S1, both six-vote ensembles improve out of sample: self-consistency by $+2.4$ points and the paraphrase ensemble by $+4.9$ (95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals $+1.1$ to $+3.7$ and $+2.3$ to $+7.6$; Holm-adjusted $p \le 0.001$), with the twelve-vote combination at $+4.1$; the two sources remain statistically inseparable ($+2.5$, interval touching zero), and $+1.9$ points of the paraphrase headline is a single-call difficulty offset, leaving $+3.0$ of within-family aggregation against $+2.4$ for resampling. The margin is conditional: it concentrates where answer-space diversity is exhausted (computation-deterministic tasks), reverses where paraphrases distort verbal-reasoning stems, and its realizing allocation is model-dependent. On saturated benchmarks, answer distributions collapse, no strategy moves accuracy, and agreement-based uncertainty estimates lose their signal. Greedy divergence from S1 is endpoint-specific, four to eleven points on three of ten configurations. An adaptive probe-and-route rule transfers at 79--87 percent of the six-call budget, beating every fixed allocation on one model by one question and trailing the best fixed conditions elsewhere by one to seven; a sequential unanimity rule preserves accuracy within one question at two to three calls. We release prompts, paraphrases, per-call logs, and recomputation scripts.
\end{abstract}
