\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Principal findings}

The study yields four principal findings. First, the effectiveness of every test-time aggregation strategy is governed by a precondition that the literature rarely states explicitly: the model must produce diverse answers at all. For the flagship models in our pool, five of six benchmarks sit in a degenerate regime in which six stochastic samples and six paraphrases return the same answer for almost every question, and in that regime self-consistency, paraphrase ensembling, and the combined ensemble reproduce both single-call baselines exactly, with the hybrid allocation differing by at most one question. This part of the degeneracy result is structural rather than empirical: when all votes coincide, every aggregation of them returns the same answer by construction. The empirical content lies in how common the coincidence is, and in its consequence that agreement-based uncertainty scores are uninformative exactly there. Second, measured against the correct $k{=}1$ baseline, the single-sample expectation S1, the two diversity sources deliver small, reliable, and statistically inseparable aggregation gains on the held-out suite: pooled over its four fully covered models (476 model--question pairs), self-consistency gains $+2.4$ points over S1, the paraphrase ensemble $+4.9$, and the twelve-vote combination $+4.1$, all with intervals excluding zero and surviving Holm correction within the designated primary family (Section~\ref{sec:method}); the paraphrase margin over self-consistency ($+2.5$, CI $-$0.6 to $+5.7$) does not separate from zero, and after removing the paraphrase difficulty margin ($+1.9$ at the single-call level) the like-for-like aggregation comparison is $+3.0$ against $+2.4$ --- roughly a factor of 1.25, not the factor of two that the raw headline suggests. On the development suite, whose per-model coverage was truncated unevenly (12 to 90 questions), the same pooled comparison sits at noise (SC-6 $+0.9$, PE-6 $-$0.9 over 112 pairs), and the per-model gains measured against greedy decoding are largely artifacts of a greedy reference that diverges from the sampled expectation on specific endpoints; we therefore treat the held-out suite as the primary evidence and the development suite as the design phase whose role was to fix the conditions, strata, and rules. Where the modal answer is wrong, the two sources pull in opposite directions, and the sign of the margin is model- and dataset-conditional: the paraphrase vote wins where resampling is exhausted, as on computation-deterministic tasks, and reverses where paraphrases shift verbal semantics (LogiQA for one model, PIQA for another), and the allocation that realizes the gain varies across models. Third, paraphrase agreement functions as an error signal in its own right, and its error coverage overlaps only partially with that of sample agreement. The development suite could support this reading only descriptively --- its informative models carried one to three ensemble errors, below the five-error rule of Section~\ref{sec:method} --- but the held-out suite recomputes it with twelve to twenty-one errors per model and confirms it: of the 72 pooled errors, sample dissent flags 49 and paraphrase dissent 46, with fifteen caught only by the sample side and twelve only by the paraphrase side; the stronger single signal tracks the model's stronger diversity source (paraphrase agreement on the two Agnes models, sample agreement on the two DeepSeek tiers); and the summed disagreement score, which the development suite could not endorse, is the strongest pooled detector on the fresh questions (AUROC 0.81 against 0.75 and 0.74). The usable signal is nevertheless mostly a regime signal: with the definitional degenerate stratum excluded, per-model AUROCs fall to between 0.49 and 0.86, so agreement separates risky-regime questions from safe ones --- a 35\% error rate where any vote dissents against 3.6\% where none does, pooled --- far better than it ranks risk within the risky set. Fourth, fragility, the fraction of questions whose answer flips under paraphrasing alone, tracks answer disagreement, concentrates in stem-locus paraphrasing (17.4\% pooled flips on the held-out suite against 9.6\% under instruction variation, with the stem-locus flips again centred on LogiQA), and appears in the same model-task cells that carry disagreement, which supports the interpretation that paraphrase disagreement and sampling disagreement are two observable symptoms of the same underlying instability. The development suite's stronger claim --- that instruction-locus flips never occur --- proved to be a property of its question draw rather than a structural law: on the held-out set, instruction-locus flips appear wherever the instruction variants differ in elicitation quality, almost entirely on SAT-Math, which is also where the instruction-variant control of Section~\ref{sec:expand} locates a single-variant effect larger than the aggregation margins.

\subsection{A practical allocation rule, and how it performs}

The regime structure suggests a simple procedure for practitioners with a fixed per-question budget. Probe the question first with a small number of calls, compute sample agreement and paraphrase agreement, and branch on the result. If both agreement scores equal one, additional calls of either kind are unlikely to change the answer, and the budget is better spent elsewhere; the degenerate regime is identifiable from a handful of calls at negligible cost. If sample agreement is high but paraphrase agreement is low, the model is prompt-brittle, and the remaining budget should go to paraphrases. If the reverse holds, the budget should go to resampling. If both scores are low, split the remaining budget between the two sources.

We evaluated this rule by simulation on the collected call matrices, charging the probe's four calls (two canonical samples and two paraphrases) to the adaptive condition's budget. The adaptive rule matches the best fixed six-call strategy on four of the six models and trails it by exactly one question on the other two (Qwen3.8-Omni and Agnes2.5-Flash), while spending a mean of 4.1 to 4.7 calls per question, 68 to 78 percent of the six-call budget; where it wins, it wins against a misallocated fixed budget, as for GPT-6-Luna, where it returns 86.7\% at a mean cost of 4.5 calls against 73.3\% for fixed SC-6. Stated against the fixed paraphrase ensemble rather than the best fixed strategy, the rule's realized allocation equals PE-6's accuracy on five of the six models at 68 to 78 percent of its calls, sitting one question below it on Qwen3.8-Omni, so its measured increment over simply running PE-6 is the call saving rather than accuracy; the hedge it buys is robustness against the opposite allocation error, at the price of forgoing SC-6's one-question edge on Agnes2.5-Flash. Because the rule was designed and evaluated on the same collected matrices, this simulation is a consistency check, not a validation; the held-out set of Section~\ref{sec:expand} supplies the transfer test, and there the rule's behaviour is model-conditional rather than uniformly conservative: on three of the four fully covered models it transfers in budget but not in allocation --- it reproduces SC-6 exactly at 86 percent of the six-call budget on Agnes3.0-Flash while sitting seven questions below its PE-6, because the regime that rewards paraphrase allocation is exactly the one its two-call probe routes to the hybrid vote; it trails PE-6 by two questions on Agnes2.5-Flash at 79 percent; and on the pro-tier DeepSeek it sits one question below the combined ensemble at 85 percent --- while on DeepSeekV4.1-Flash it exceeds every fixed allocation (84.0 percent at 87 percent of budget, one question above the best of them), the only instance in either suite where the probe converts into a win over the whole fixed frontier. Across the development suite's six models, an even simpler sequential rule that draws canonical samples until the first unanimity and otherwise falls back to the six-sample majority preserves SC-6 accuracy within one question everywhere at a mean budget of two to three calls; this is a special case of the early-stopping self-consistency family \citep{aggarwal2023lets,li2024escape}, and our simulation reproduces that family's finding in the direct-answering, near-degenerate regime. Because agreement statistics are cheap to compute online, the rules add no model training and no human labeling, and both simulations are reproducible from the released logs. The hybrid allocation, by contrast, is best understood as the fixed-budget version of the same hedge: it matches the best pure strategy when one source fails and trails it by about one question when both work, so the adaptive probe matches it in our data at two-thirds to three-quarters of the cost.

\subsection{Implications for evaluation and for uncertainty estimation}

Two methodological consequences follow. For benchmark evaluation, our results add an aggregation dimension to the multi-prompt argument of Mizrahi et al. \citep{mizrahi2024state}: the same question answered under one prompt with one decode, under six paraphrases, or under twelve combined calls yields systematically different accuracies in the high-entropy regime, so reported scores should state the aggregation configuration, and comparisons across papers that aggregate differently are not like for like. The single-sample baseline S1 deserves the same reporting discipline, because greedy decoding and a single stochastic draw differ by four to eleven points on four of the ten model--endpoint configurations we measured --- three with greedy below the sampled expectation and one above --- and are statistically indistinguishable on the rest, including three of the four held-out endpoints; the divergence is an endpoint property to be checked per deployment, not a universal offset. For uncertainty estimation, the degenerate regime is a warning whose scope we state precisely. Our measurements cover multiple-choice questions answered directly, where sampling-based confidence measures, including simple agreement heuristics, are uninformative when the sample distribution collapses, and our data show that this collapse is the common case for strong models on saturated tasks. Semantic-entropy methods for free-form generation \citep{kuhn2023semantic,farquhar2024detecting} face an analogous risk when the sampled paraphrase classes collapse, but our design does not test that setting, and the caution transfers by argument rather than by measurement. Paraphrase probes partially restore the signal because they perturb the input rather than the decoder, but they too inherit the collapse when the model is robust in both views, as our flagship models are on five of six benchmarks. Confidence methods should therefore be validated per regime rather than averaged over mixed test sets, and deployments should verify that the answer distribution carries variance before trusting agreement-based scores.

\subsection{Limitations}

Eight limitations bound the scope of our conclusions. First, sample size and power: fifteen questions per benchmark and completed sets of 12 to 90 questions per model support paired within-model comparisons of large effects, but the cluster-bootstrap intervals are wide, TOST does not establish equivalence for most pairs, and differences of a few points between strategies would go undetected; the 119-question held-out set of Section~\ref{sec:expand} multiplies per-model coverage for four systems (two Agnes generations and two DeepSeek tiers), while two of its six systems remain partially covered, and scaling each benchmark to hundreds of questions remains the single change most likely to sharpen the conclusions. Second, provider rate limits and free-tier quotas constrained coverage unevenly across models; the quota of Qwen3.8-Omni was exhausted before its follow-up conditions could be run, and during the expansion collection the shared workspace serving Qwen3.7-Flash and both DeepSeek models revoked free-tier access mid-run: Qwen3.7-Flash stopped at 60 of 119 questions, DeepSeekV4.1-Flash was completed on a second free endpoint after the first died at 9 questions, and DeepSeekV4-Flash-0731 stopped at 13 when the second endpoint moved it behind a paid subscription mid-collection, an instance of the broader reproducibility hazard that free-tier access to a given model--endpoint configuration can be withdrawn at any time; coverage follows a fixed question order rather than adaptive selection, but cross-model aggregates weight models unequally. Third, the models are commercial systems accessed through third-party endpoints, one of which --- the Apinex relay that served GPT-6-Luna, Gemini3.8-Flash, and the completed DeepSeek runs --- is an aggregator rather than a first-party API: we can neither verify that a relay serves the requested model identity and version against the provider's own API nor confirm that the requested temperature is honored, especially for reasoning-style models, and decoding internals, post-training, and version pinning are outside our control; no condition in this study was replicated on a first-party API or an open-weight model, so all findings describe the accessed endpoints as of October 2026 and need not transfer to other versions or serving configurations. The greedy-versus-S1 gap documented in Section~\ref{sec:rq1}, including its truncation artifact, is itself a manifestation of this limitation. We release per-call timestamps, token usage, and raw outputs so that the configuration is auditable, and each provider's published model card or technical report is cited per system (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); an official-API or open-weight replication remains the cleanest resolution and is left open. Fourth, paraphrases were generated by two models and validated by embedding similarity with a fixed threshold plus a bidirectional entailment check, but semantic equivalence of adversarial questions is not fully formalizable, and stricter thresholds or human-curated paraphrases could shift the fragility statistics; a related caveat is that Qwen3.7-Flash, the stem-paraphrase generator, is itself evaluated in the held-out suite, so its paraphrase-condition cells carry a self-preference risk, and its partial coverage (60 of 119) keeps them out of the pooled analyses. Two controls bound these readings only partially: the greedy-paraphrase control that separates prompt-space variation from decoding noise was collected on the development suite only, so held-out paraphrase margins retain that mixing (\ref{app:greedypara}), and the best-instruction self-consistency condition for the SAT-Math cell was not run, with Section~\ref{sec:expand} bounding it instead through a post-hoc variant analysis. Fifth, the multiple-choice tasks use direct answering while GSM8K uses chain of thought; the aggregation comparison is internally valid within each answering mode, and the follow-up condition of \ref{app:cot} measures the mode effect directly, but absolute accuracies are not comparable across modes. Sixth, the high-dissent regime is sampled through fewer model-task cells than the degenerate regime, resting primarily on logical reasoning and selected quantitative tasks; the MMLU-Pro probe of \ref{app:mmlupro} begins to broaden this, at small scale. Seventh, error-detection metrics are computed against ensemble references that share votes with the detectors, and the adaptive allocation rule was designed and evaluated on the same development-suite matrices, so both carry a same-data caveat; the held-out set mitigates but does not eliminate it, because the strata and the rule were fixed after the development-suite results were known; we separate the definitional degenerate stratum and report disjoint-vote correlations, and the held-out set of Section~\ref{sec:expand} now provides the fresh-question evaluation of the allocation rule, but an error reference from fully independent draws would still be cleaner. Eighth, all conditions use a fixed budget of six or twelve calls with majority voting; ranked voting, weighted aggregation, or learned vote combination could change the budget-matched comparison.

\subsection{Future work}

Three extensions follow naturally. The adaptive allocation rule can be moved online, interleaving probing and allocation per question rather than simulating the split afterwards, and its threshold can be tuned against a target budget rather than fixed at unanimity. The comparison can be extended to free-form generation, where semantic clustering replaces majority voting and paraphrase ensembles interact with surface-form competition effects \citep{holtzman2021surface}, and where the degeneracy phenomenon has direct consequences for semantic-entropy hallucination detection that our multiple-choice design cannot measure. Finally, the fragility statistic can serve as a benchmark diagnostic in its own right, complementing accuracy with a measure of how strongly a model's answers depend on the surface form of the question.
