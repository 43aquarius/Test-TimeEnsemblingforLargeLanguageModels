\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a budget-matched comparison of the two diversity sources available to test-time ensembling for large language models: resampling the decoder on a fixed prompt, and perturbing the prompt across validated semantic paraphrases. A sixteen-call matrix per question per model, evaluated on six models from four families and six benchmarks, allowed every condition, including self-consistency, paraphrase ensembling, a hybrid allocation, and a combined ensemble, to be derived from matched calls on identical questions, and follow-up controls isolated the prompt-space contribution with greedy paraphrase decodes, probed harder tasks on MMLU-Pro, and varied the answering mode with chain-of-thought instructions. The results organize into a regime structure. Where models are confident, answer distributions collapse, all aggregation strategies coincide with greedy decoding, and consistency-based uncertainty estimation loses its signal. Where answer entropy exists, both diversity sources improve accuracy, paraphrase agreement flags errors that sample agreement misses and vice versa, and the hybrid allocation hedges: it matches the best pure strategy when one source fails, at a cost of about one question when both work. No significant difference between the two diversity sources was detected at matched budgets, and the study is explicit that its sample sizes do not establish equivalence. The practical takeaway is to measure agreement first and allocate the budget accordingly: an adaptive rule simulated on the collected matrices matches or exceeds every fixed strategy at two-thirds of the budget, and a sequential unanimity rule preserves self-consistency accuracy at a mean cost of two to three calls. The methodological takeaway is that aggregation configuration, and the choice between greedy and single-sample baselines, belong in the reporting standard of every evaluation that uses them. We release prompts, paraphrases, and raw predictions to support replication as model behavior continues to shift.
